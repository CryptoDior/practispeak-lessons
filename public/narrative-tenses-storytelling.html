Vocabulary
8 words for telling epic gaming stories
0 1
COMEBACK
Noun

A recovery from a losing or disadvantaged position to win — one of the most exciting narratives in competitive gaming.

Example

"We were down three rounds with no economy — the comeback we pulled off was the greatest I had ever been part of."

0 2
AMBUSH
Noun / Verb

A surprise attack from a hidden position — the attacker had been waiting while the victim had no idea what was coming.

Example

"I was running through the corridor when they ambushed us from both sides — we had walked right into their trap."

0 3
OUTPLAY
Verb

To defeat an opponent through superior skill, decision-making, or positioning — not luck. The heart of every great gaming story.

Example

"They outplayed us completely in the final round — every decision they made was a step ahead of what we expected."

0 4
MOMENTUM
Noun

The build-up of energy or advantage that carries a team forward — losing momentum often marks the turning point of a match story.

Example

"We had all the momentum going into the final round — and then one mistake shattered it completely."

0 5
HIGHLIGHT
Noun

A particularly impressive, exciting, or memorable moment in a game — the kind of moment that gets clipped, shared, and retold.

Example

"That snipe was the highlight of the entire tournament — people were still talking about it two weeks later."

0 6
NARRATE
Verb

To tell the story of what happened — describing events in sequence, giving context, and bringing the listener into the moment.

Example

"He narrated the entire match from memory — every rotation, every mistake, every moment that changed the outcome."

0 7
TURNING POINT
Noun

The critical moment in a match where the direction of the game begins to change — every great story has one.

Example

"The turning point was when they lost their captain — after that, their whole strategy fell apart."

0 8
ANTICIPATE
Verb

To predict or expect what is about to happen before it does — anticipating the enemy's move is the difference between a highlight and an obituary.

Example

"I had anticipated their push — I was already in position before they even crossed the door."

Phrases
8 phrases for building a narrative — opening, sequencing, backgrounding, and reflecting
0 1
"It all started when…"
Opening a Story — Past Simple

The classic story opener — it signals you're about to go back to the beginning and take the listener through what happened step by step. Creates immediate engagement.

Example

"It all started when we queued into a lobby we almost didn't join — the match that changed everything for our team."

0 2
"I was just about to… when…"
Interrupted Action — Past Continuous

Sets up an action that was in progress — then something interrupts it. This is one of the most powerful narrative structures: it builds suspense right before the key moment lands.

Example

"I was just about to push through the door when the explosion went off — three of us went down instantly."

0 3
"While they were…, we were…"
Parallel Actions — Past Continuous

Shows two things happening at the same time — crucial for explaining how a trap was set, how a strategy unfolded, or why both sides were caught off guard simultaneously.

Example

"While they were pushing the left flank, we were quietly setting up the ambush on the right — they never saw it coming."

0 4
"As soon as I…"
Sequencing Events — Past Simple

Connects two actions where one immediately triggers the other — essential for building pace in a story. Creates the feeling of rapid, decisive events in sequence.

Example

"As soon as I heard the footsteps, I swapped to my secondary and held the angle — it was pure instinct."

0 5
"By the time we got there,…"
Prior Completion — Past Perfect

Shows that something had already happened before another event. Essential for explaining the situation that greeted you when you arrived — the damage was already done.

Example

"By the time we got there, they had already taken the objective and fortified every entry point — we had nothing to work with."

0 6
"We had already…"
Completed Before the Main Event — Past Perfect

Explains what your team had done in preparation — before the key moment of the story happened. Shows foresight, planning, or unfortunately, a mistake made too early.

Example

"We had already used our only revive — so when the last player went down, there was nothing left to do."

0 7
"That's when everything changed."
Pivoting the Narrative

The single most powerful sentence in storytelling — it marks the turning point where everything before leads to everything after. Use it to split your story in two: before and after.

Example

"We were down to one player, no resources, one second on the clock. That's when everything changed."

0 8
"Looking back, I should have…"
Reflecting on Decisions — Past Modal

Steps outside the story to reflect — you're no longer in the moment, you're analysing it. The past modal "should have" shows what the better decision would have been. Honest, relatable, and powerful.

Example

"Looking back, I should have held my position instead of pushing — I had all the information I needed to make the right call."

💡 Grammar Focus — The Three Narrative Tenses

Stories use three past tenses working together. Each one has a specific job. Using only one tense makes a story flat — mixing all three makes it vivid, layered, and professional.

Past Simple — the spine

Form: verb + -ed / irregular
Job: completed actions in sequence
"I pushed in. I took the shot. We won."
This is the main timeline of your story — events listed in order.

Past Continuous — the backdrop

Form: was/were + verb-ing
Job: background actions / interruptions
"I was holding the angle when they pushed."
Sets the scene. The action that was already in progress when something happened.

Past Perfect — the backstory

Form: had + past participle
Job: what happened BEFORE the main story
"They had already taken the objective."
Goes further back in time. The past before the past.

Gaming examples — all three tenses together

"I was watching the minimap (past cont.) when I noticed (past simple) that they had flanked us (past perfect) from behind." → three tenses, one sentence
"While they were pushing the objective (past cont.), we had already set up the ambush (past perfect). As soon as they entered (past simple), we opened fire." → full narrative sequence
"I was lining up the shot (past cont.) when my teammate called out (past simple) that two enemies had passed behind us (past perfect)." → interruption + backstory
"By the time I got back from AFK (past simple), they had scored three times (past perfect) and we were already losing (past cont.) the momentum." → arrival + what had happened + ongoing state
"It all started (past simple) when we were grinding ranked (past cont.) and I realised (past simple) I had seen (past perfect) this pattern before." → opening a story with all three
"Looking back, I should have rotated earlier (past modal) — while I was sitting in position (past cont.), they had secured (past perfect) every advantage they needed." → reflection with tense contrast
⚠️ The most common mistake: using past simple for everything. "I was in position. The enemy came. I shot. I missed. We lost." — this is technically correct but reads like a list, not a story. Mix the tenses to show what was already happening, what happened next, and what had already occurred before that moment.
In Action
How narrative tenses appear across different gaming storytelling contexts
TwitchStreamer Retelling a Clutch Moment

"Okay so let me tell you what happened, because chat missed it. It all started when I rotated to the wrong building — I had misread the zone and put myself in a terrible position. While they were pushing from the north, I was completely alone with no cover and two squads closing in. I was just about to call it and take the loss when I remembered the balcony I had seen two minutes earlier. As soon as I got up there, I could see everything. I dropped three before they even knew where I was. By the time the last squad arrived, I had already reset and was waiting. That's when everything changed."

Notice how the streamer uses past continuous to set the scene ("while they were pushing"), past perfect to explain prior context ("I had misread the zone"), and past simple to deliver each action in sequence. The mix creates energy and keeps the listener hooked.
YouTubeHighlight Reel Narration

"This clip is from the most insane comeback we have ever pulled off. We had already lost the first two rounds and our economy was completely destroyed — we had nothing. While the other team was celebrating in the lobby, we were rebuilding our strategy from scratch. Every single player bought the cheapest weapon available. By the time round three started, they had forgotten we were even a threat. As soon as the round began, we executed the play we had rehearsed a hundred times in practice. They never anticipated it. Looking back, I think we should have played that aggressive style from the beginning — but sometimes you need to hit rock bottom before you find what actually works."

YouTube narration is more structured than live commentary — the storyteller can build up slowly, use past perfect to explain the backstory, and reflect at the end. Notice how "Looking back" signals the shift from storytelling to analysis.
DiscordTeammate Retelling the Final Round

"Bro, you were AFK for the best part. So basically — while you were gone, we were holding a 4v5 for three rounds straight. We had already used every advantage we had just to stay in the game. It all started going wrong when we lost the third player — after that it was basically survival mode. I was just about to call a retreat when Kai held a one-versus-three from that corner — you know the one. As soon as he got the first kill, the momentum completely shifted. By the time you came back, we had won two of the three rounds you missed. Looking back, I still don't know how we pulled that off."

Discord storytelling is the most casual — contractions, informal address ("bro"), and a mix of all three tenses without the listener ever noticing. Great storytelling feels natural even when it's grammatically complex.
Register
How storytelling style shifts across different gaming contexts
ContextRegisterNarrative Style
Live stream (in the moment)
Twitch, YouTube Live		 Informal / Spontaneous "Okay so — I was literally just about to die and then — wait, chat, let me explain from the start."
Post-game voice chat
Discord, party chat		 Informal / Energetic "Bro, while you were away we were holding a 4v5. By the time you came back we'd already won two rounds."
YouTube highlight narration
Edited video, scripted		 Neutral / Structured "It all started when we entered the lobby without a clear strategy. We had already lost two rounds by the time the turning point arrived."
Match review / coaching session
Analytical, educational		 Formal / Analytical "At this point in the round, the team was rotating to B while the opponents had already secured A. As soon as the rotation began, the advantage was lost."
Esports journalism
Written article, match report		 Formal / Professional "The turning point arrived in round twelve. While the defending team was repositioning, the attackers had already identified the gap and executed a coordinated ambush."
✅All 8 phrases work across every context — from casual Discord stories to formal match reports. What changes is the vocabulary and speed, not the tense structures themselves.
💡"That's when everything changed" works in every register — it's equally powerful in a casual Discord retell and in professional esports journalism. It's one of the most universal storytelling pivots in English.
⚠️In formal match reports, contract "we'd" to "we had" and "they'd" to "they had". Contractions with past perfect are fine in speech but look informal in written analysis.
❌Don't use present tense to tell past stories — "So I go in and I shoot and then he kills me" is common in spoken English but sounds awkward in written form. In writing, always commit to the past narrative tenses.
In Context
Read the match story — highlighted phrases are from this lesson
Match Debrief — "The Round We Almost Threw"

"It all started when we made the decision to split our team — two players pushing A, three holding B. Nobody had called it out clearly, so there was already confusion before the round even began.

While the A-side players were pushing forward, the B-side was getting picked off one by one. We had no idea. By the time we heard the callout, two of our teammates had already been eliminated and one was holding on by a thread. We had already committed to the push — there was no way back.

I was just about to call a full retreat when the last remaining player on B pulled off something I had never seen before. As soon as she flanked right, the opponents reacted — and that single movement exposed their entire backline. That's when everything changed.

We won that round. We won the match. Looking back, I should have made the callout at the start — the split strategy was right, but the communication was wrong. We got lucky. Great teams don't rely on luck. They rely on clarity."

Comprehension Questions

Think about why each tense is used — not just what it means.

1. "While the A-side players were pushing forward, the B-side was getting picked off." Why are both verbs in past continuous?
A
Because both things happened a long time ago
B
Because both actions were ongoing at the same time — past continuous shows two parallel background events happening simultaneously
C
Because neither action was completed successfully
D
Because past continuous is more polite than past simple
2. "By the time we heard the callout, two players had already been eliminated." Why is "had been eliminated" in past perfect?
A
Because it was a very dramatic event
B
Because the eliminations happened BEFORE the team heard the callout — past perfect shows the earlier event in a sequence
C
Because the players were not on the team's side
D
Because "had been" is more formal than "were"
3. "I was just about to call a full retreat when the last player pulled off something remarkable." What does the past continuous + past simple structure create here?
A
It shows that both actions were equally important
B
It creates suspense — the ongoing action ("was about to") is interrupted at the last possible moment by the sudden past simple event
C
It shows the narrator was not paying attention
D
It signals that the retreat did eventually happen later
4. The narrator ends with "Looking back, I should have made the callout at the start." What does this shift in language tell us?
A
The narrator is angry at the team and is blaming them
B
The narrator is no longer telling the story — they have stepped outside it to reflect and analyse what went wrong with honest self-criticism
C
The narrator is suggesting the team should replay the match
D
The narrator forgot what they were going to say and changed topic
Speaking
Choose a scenario — tell your story, debate, or narrate live
Pick one option. You have 3–4 minutes to prepare and speak. Use at least 4 phrases from the lesson — and make sure you use all three narrative tenses at least once each. Don't narrate in a list — build a real story.
🎮Option A

Tell Your Story

Recount your best — or worst — gaming moment. Use all three narrative tenses naturally. Your partner listens and asks two follow-up questions when you finish. Then swap.

⚖️Option B

Debate: Highlights vs Consistency

Your partner argues: "A player's highlight reel matters more than their consistency." Disagree — build a counter-argument using storytelling examples and at least three phrases from this lesson. Then swap sides.

🎙️Option C

Commentary Replay

You are watching back a clip of your own match. Narrate what you see — what was happening before the key moment, what you were doing wrong, what changed, and what you should have done differently. Use all three tenses and end with "looking back."

Useful reminders while you speak

  • Start with past simple to set the scene quickly — then use past continuous to slow down and describe what was happening in the background.
  • Use past perfect to drop in the backstory: what had already happened before the moment you're describing.
  • "That's when everything changed" is your pivot — everything before it is build-up, everything after is resolution.
  • End with reflection: "Looking back, I should have…" shows maturity and self-awareness — in gaming and in English.
