Phrases
8 phrases for reading esports news and reporting with passive voice
📰
0 1
"It was announced that…"
Reporting News

Opens a news report without naming who made the announcement. The passive voice keeps the focus on the news itself, not the source — standard in professional journalism and esports reporting.

Example

"It was announced that the world championship would be held in Seoul for the second year in a row."

⚖️
0 2
"The player was banned for…"
Reporting a Punishment

Reports a disciplinary action using the passive voice. The focus is on the player and the reason — not on who issued the ban. This structure is used in every esports news story involving rule violations.

Example

"The player was banned for six months after testing positive for a prohibited substance during the tournament."

🎯
0 3
"The match was decided by…"
Describing an Outcome

Describes how a match ended using passive voice. Puts the outcome and cause first — essential for match reports and post-event analysis where the key factor matters more than who did what.

Example

"The match was decided by a single mistake in the final round — a missed shot that cost the team everything."

🔍
0 4
"It is believed that…"
Hedging in News

Used when a journalist reports information that is not yet confirmed. The passive voice distances the writer from the claim — they're reporting what others think, not stating it as fact. Critical for responsible reporting.

Example

"It is believed that the star player's contract was not renewed, though neither side has confirmed this officially."

✍️
0 5
"The team has been signed by…"
Transfer News

Reports a player or team signing using the present perfect passive. The focus is on the team being signed — not on the organisation doing the signing. Used in every transfer announcement across esports.

Example

"The team has been signed by one of the largest esports organisations in North America in a multi-year deal."

🔧
0 6
"The patch was introduced to…"
Explaining Changes

Explains why a game update was made, using passive voice to put the focus on the patch and its purpose — not on the developers who made it. Standard in game analysis and esports journalism.

Example

"The patch was introduced to address the dominant strategy that had made the last three tournaments predictable and repetitive."

📎
0 7
"According to reports…"
Citing a Source

Attributes information to an unnamed or general source — giving credibility to the claim without needing to identify a specific person. Common in breaking news and transfers where sources are confidential.

Example

"According to reports, the organisation has been in negotiations with three international players for the past month."

📅
0 8
"The tournament is expected to…"
Future Passive

Reports anticipated future events without stating them as certain facts. The passive form sounds objective and journalistic — this is how upcoming events, prize pools, and schedules are discussed in esports media.

Example

"The tournament is expected to attract over a million live viewers and offer a prize pool of five million dollars."

💡 Grammar Focus — The Passive Voice

The passive voice puts the action or result at the front of the sentence — not the person doing it. This is why news and journalism use it constantly: sometimes the subject is unknown, unimportant, or deliberately withheld.

Present Passive

Pattern: is / are + past participle
"The player is suspended."
"Teams are seeded by ranking."
Used for current states and ongoing situations.

Past Passive

Pattern: was / were + past participle
"The player was banned."
"The match was decided in overtime."
Used to report completed events — the backbone of match reports.

Perfect Passive

Pattern: has / have been + past participle
"The team has been signed."
"Three players have been released."
Used for recent news — something just happened, still relevant now.

Active → Passive — gaming examples

Active: "The organisation signed the player." → Passive: "The player was signed by the organisation." → focus shifts to the player
Active: "Developers released a major patch." → Passive: "A major patch was released." → who released it doesn't matter
Active: "The judges disqualified the team." → Passive: "The team was disqualified." → focus on the consequence, not the judges
Active: "Three sponsors dropped the player." → Passive: "The player was dropped by three sponsors." → passive emphasises the player's loss
Active: "Nobody has confirmed the rumour." → Passive: "The rumour has not been confirmed." → objective, journalistic tone
Active: "People expect the tournament to break viewership records." → Passive: "The tournament is expected to break viewership records." → sounds authoritative without naming who expects it
Active: "They broadcast the final to 5 million viewers." → Passive: "The final was broadcast to 5 million viewers." → the audience size is the headline, not who broadcast it
⚠️ Why does passive voice dominate esports news? Three reasons: (1) the action is more important than who did it, (2) the source is confidential or unknown, (3) it sounds more formal and objective. When you read esports articles, look for passive structures — you'll find them in almost every paragraph.

According to reports, contract negotiations had broken down over salary disputes that began three months ago. Notice how the entire article is written in passive voice — the journalist never says "we think" or "they told us." Passive voice makes news sound official and objective, even when the information comes from anonymous sources.

Reddit Community Post — Post-Match Analysis

"People keep asking what went wrong in the final. Here's the honest breakdown: the match was decided by a single draft decision in game three — they picked into a losing composition and never recovered. The patch was introduced to weaken exactly the style of play they relied on, and they hadn't adapted their strategy at all. The player was banned from the last qualifier, so this was their first major event without him. The roster showed gaps. It's that simple. They were seeded first, and nobody expects to lose to a team ranked eighth. But that's esports."

Reddit analysis uses passive voice more loosely — mixing it with active sentences for rhythm. Notice how passive structures still carry the key analytical points: the draft decision, the patch change, the ban. Active sentences handle opinion and colour.
Image placeholder — 16:9
Live Commentary Tournament Caster — Final Round

"And we are moments away from the end of this incredible final. This tournament was expected to be close — nobody predicted it would come down to the wire like this. The position has been taken, the objective has been secured, and the crowd here is on their feet. According to the analysts, this was the play all along — they had prepared this exact rotation in practice. Live casters use passive voice to build drama — "the position has been taken", "the title is about to be claimed." It slows the pace and creates tension. Compare this to how quickly active sentences land in contrast: notice every passive sets up an active punch.

✅ All 8 phrases work naturally in written esports journalism. They sound professional and objective — exactly the tone that separates real reporting from fan opinion.
💡 "It is believed that…" and "According to reports…" are your most important hedging tools. Use them when you don't have confirmed facts — they protect you from stating something unverified as truth.
⚠️ On social media, passive voice is often compressed to the point of omitting "to be": "Player dropped" instead of "The player was dropped." This is acceptable on Twitter but would look unprofessional in a formal article.
❌ Don't overuse passive voice in live commentary — it slows the energy. Active sentences like "He hits it!" land faster and harder. Passive works best for setup and context; active delivers the punch.

Esports Weekly — "Storm Rising: The Unexpected Champions of the Season"

It was announced that Storm Rising had qualified for the World Championship after defeating the defending champions in a five-game series that left the esports world in disbelief. The team, which was seeded eighth going into the playoff bracket, overcame odds of more than twenty to one to reach the final.

According to reports, the team's preparation had been exceptional — they had studied the opponent's patterns for six weeks before the event. The patch was introduced to reduce the dominance of high-damage compositions, and Storm Rising were one of the few teams that had fully adapted their roster strategy to the new meta. The match was decided by a coordinated rotation in round four that the opponents had clearly not anticipated.

It is believed that several top organisations have already reached out to the team's management following the victory. The team has been signed by a new equipment sponsor effective immediately. The tournament final replay is expected to be among the most-watched esports broadcasts of the year, with the organisation's social media following having grown by 300 percent in the 48 hours since the match ended.

Useful reminders while you speak

  • Use passive voice when the action or outcome matters more than who did it — this is the key instinct to develop.
  • In the debate, "It is believed that…" and "According to reports…" make your arguments sound sourced and credible rather than personal opinion.
  • In the press conference, "The player was released" is far more professional than "We fired him." Passive voice manages difficult news without pointing blame.
  • In commentary, mix passive (for setup and context) with sharp active sentences (for the moment of action). The contrast creates energy.
